Research question · CSE3728 Problem 9

What really determines the performance of a quantum image-processing system?

Image size, intensity representation, preprocessing, circuit structure, number of operations, measurement and reconstruction were varied in controlled experiments on Qiskit Aer. QIP-Bench measures each one's influence on quality, efficiency and reliability.

Business takeaway

Engineer the circuit first

Decomposition choices change CNOT counts by an order of magnitude for the same quantum state. That is the cheapest performance win before any hardware spend.

Budget measurements, not just qubits

Qubit count alone misleads. FRQI is qubit-cheap but its quality is shot-limited, while NEQR needs more qubits and gates but reads out exactly.

Match encoding to the noise regime

Basis encoding (NEQR) is robust until a noise threshold and then collapses. Angle encoding (FRQI) degrades gracefully. Choose by device error rate.

Resource vs quality

Quantum Hadamard Edge Detection (Yao et al., 2017): amplitude-encode the image, add one ancilla, apply H · decrement · H. Odd basis states then carry the neighbour differences (ci − ci+1)/2. It runs twice, once on the image and once on its transpose.

FRQI

|I⟩ = 1/√N Σi (cos θi|0⟩ + sin θi|1⟩) ⊗ |i⟩
θi = (π/2)·Ii

2n+1 qubits for a 2n×2n image. The optimized structure uses a Gray-code uniformly controlled RY with exactly 22n CNOTs. Decoding: θ = arcsin √P(1|i).

NEQR

|I⟩ = 1/√N Σi |Ci⟩ ⊗ |i⟩
Ci ∈ {0,1}q

2n+q qubits. Grey levels are basis states, so one observation per pixel reads them exactly. The optimized structure shares X-flips in Gray order and uses one ancilla per pixel condition.

Amplitude / QHED

|I⟩ = Σi ci|i⟩, ci = Ii/‖I‖

Only 2n qubits, but the norm is classical side-information. QHED adds one ancilla and reads every neighbour difference from the odd basis states.
